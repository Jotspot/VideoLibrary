VideoLibrary 26.10.2 is saving to your Downloads folder. Installing takes about two minutes. Follow the steps below, even if you've never installed a Mac app from outside the App Store.

Didn't start? Download it again.

  1. 1

    Unzip it

    Open your Downloads folder (in the Dock, or press ⌥⌘L in Finder) and double-click VideoLibrary.zip. A VideoLibrary app appears next to it.

    Safari sometimes unzips downloads for you. If you already see the app, skip to step 2.

  2. 2

    Move it to Applications

    Drag VideoLibrary onto Applications in the Finder sidebar. That keeps it with your other apps, and it shows up in Launchpad and Spotlight.

  3. 3

    Open it the first time

    VideoLibrary is free and open source, but it isn't registered with Apple (that costs $99 a year), so macOS stops it the first time with a message that it "could not verify" the app. That's expected. You only need to allow it once.

    The easy way: System Settings

    1. Double-click VideoLibrary in Applications. When the warning appears, click Done.
    2. Open System Settings and choose Privacy & Security in the sidebar.
    3. Scroll down to Security. Next to "VideoLibrary was blocked to protect your Mac", click Open Anyway.
    4. Enter your Mac password (or use Touch ID), then click Open Anyway once more.

    Or: one Terminal command

    Open Terminal (search for it with ⌘Space), paste this and press Return. It removes the "downloaded from the internet" flag, so the app opens normally.

    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/VideoLibrary.app
  4. 4

    Allow Keychain access

    VideoLibrary encrypts your sign-ins with a key it keeps in your Mac's Keychain. The first time it starts, macOS asks whether it may use that key. Enter your password and click Always Allow, so it doesn't ask again.

  5. 5

    Install the download tools

    VideoLibrary uses three free tools to download video: yt-dlp, FFmpeg and Deno. In the app, open Settings (⌘,) › Tools and click Install.

    This uses Homebrew. If you don't have it, paste the command from brew.sh into Terminal first, or install everything yourself:

    brew install yt-dlp ffmpeg deno
  6. 6

    Add your first video

    Click + in the toolbar and paste a link from YouTube, Nebula or any other supported site. Or open Suggestions and pick something from your feed.

Stuck?

  • No "Open Anyway" button? It only appears for about an hour after you try to open the app. Double-click VideoLibrary again, then go back to Privacy & Security.
  • "VideoLibrary is damaged and can't be opened"? Use the Terminal command in step 3. The app isn't damaged; macOS shows this for some unsigned downloads.
  • Needs macOS 26 (Tahoe) or later on an Apple silicon Mac (M1 or newer).
  • Still stuck? Open an issue on GitHub.
